## Core Theoretical Foundations

### Signaling Theory
- Spence, M. (1973). Job market signaling. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 87(3), 355-374.
  - Foundation: signals valuable because costly to produce
  - EN6 application: like = signal of attention; when cost → 0, value → 0

- Donath, J. (2007). Signals in social supernets. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, 13(1), 231-251.
  - Extends Spence to online identity signals
  - "Honest signaling" requires cost; cheap signals are unreliable

- Donath, J. (2014). *The Social Machine: Designs for Living Online*. MIT Press.
  - Chapter on identity signals and their devaluation in social media

### Information Economics & Inflation Analogy
- Akerlof, G. (1970). The market for lemons. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 84(3), 488-500.
  - Information asymmetry → market collapse. Parallel: genuine vs inflated likes → signal market collapse

- Gresham's Law (historical): Bad money drives out good
  - Application: inflated likes drive out genuine engagement motivation

- Varian, H. (2010). *Intermediate Microeconomics*. W.W. Norton.
  - Information goods economics framework

## Engagement Decline Data & Analysis

### Instagram Engagement Rate
- Rival IQ. (2016-2025). *Social Media Industry Benchmark Reports* (annual).
  - Instagram median engagement rate by industry, 2015-2025
  - Key data: 1.22% (2015) → 0.70% (2018) → 0.36% (2024)

- Socialinsider. (2020-2025). *Instagram Engagement Study* (annual).
  - Engagement rate by account size, post type, industry
  - Corroborates Rival IQ trend

- Later & Fohr. (2019). *The State of Instagram Marketing*.
  - Engagement rate decline analysis with cohort breakdown

### Facebook Organic Reach
- Edgerank Checker. (2013). "Facebook Has Decreased Organic Reach."
  - First systematic documentation of organic reach decline
  - 16% → 12% (2012-2013)

- Social@Ogilvy. (2014). "Facebook Zero: Considering Life After the Demise of Organic Reach."
  - Organic reach: 6.15% (Feb 2014) → 2.6% (projected)
  - Framed as "pay to play" shift

- Locowise. (2015-2018). Monthly Facebook page analytics reports.
  - Organic reach → <2% by 2016, <1.5% by 2018

### Platform DAU/MAU/Revenue
- Meta Platforms, Inc. SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q). 2012-2025.
  - FB DAU/MAU ratio, revenue, ARPU (already collected for EN1: data/meta_quarterly.csv)

- Snap Inc. SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q). 2017-2025.
  - Snapchat DAU, revenue (already collected for EN1: data/snap_quarterly.csv)

## Like-Hiding & Signal Deprecation

### Instagram Like-Hiding
- Instagram Blog. (2019, July). "Testing Private Like Counts."
  - Initial test in Canada, then expanded to Australia, Brazil, Ireland, Italy, Japan, New Zealand

- Mosseri, A. (2019). Twitter post: "We want people to worry a little less about how many likes they get on Instagram and spend a bit more time connecting with the people that they care about."

- Instagram Blog. (2021, May). "Giving People More Control on Instagram."
  - Global rollout of optional like-hiding

- Apaolaza, V., et al. (2023). "Hiding like counts on Instagram: Effects on well-being and engagement." *Computers in Human Behavior*, 139, 107531.
  - Empirical study on like-hiding effects

### YouTube Dislike-Hiding
- YouTube Blog. (2021, November). "An Update to Dislikes on YouTube."
  - Public dislike counts removed
  - Stated reason: "dislike attacks" and creator wellbeing

### Twitter/X (No Deprecation Case)
- Elon Musk's reversal of signal deprecation (re-emphasis on metrics)
  - Useful as control case: platform that did NOT deprecate signals

## Mental Health & Like Toxification

### Social Comparison & Likes
- Vogel, E. A., et al. (2014). "Social comparison, social media, and self-esteem." *Psychology of Popular Media Culture*, 3(4), 206-222.
  - Upward social comparison on Facebook → decreased self-esteem

- Appel, H., et al. (2016). "The interplay between Facebook use, social comparison, envy, and depression." *Current Opinion in Psychology*, 9, 44-49.
  - Meta-review of like-count → comparison → depression pathway

- Hawi, N. S., & Samaha, M. (2017). "The relations among social media addiction, self-esteem, and life satisfaction in university students." *Social Science Computer Review*, 35(5), 576-586.

### Platform-Specific Research
- Royal Society for Public Health. (2017). *#StatusOfMind: Social media and young people's mental health and wellbeing*.
  - Instagram ranked worst for young people's mental health

- Wells, G., Horwitz, J., & Seetharaman, D. (2021). "Facebook Knows Instagram Is Toxic for Teen Girls, Company Documents Show." *Wall Street Journal*.
  - Internal Facebook research: "We make body image issues worse for one in three teen girls"

- Braghieri, L., Levy, R., & Makarin, A. (2022). "Social media and mental health." *American Economic Review*, 112(11), 3660-3693.
  - Quasi-experimental evidence: Facebook rollout → mental health decline
  - Uses staggered university-level rollout as instrument

## Stories, Ephemeral Media & Signal Restoration

### Stories & Ephemeral Sharing
- Bayer, J. B., et al. (2016). "Sharing the small moments: Ephemeral social interaction on Snapchat." *Information, Communication & Society*, 19(7), 956-977.
  - Snapchat's ephemeral design → more authentic sharing

- Vaterlaus, J. M., et al. (2016). "Snapchat is more personal: An exploratory study on Snapchat behaviors and young adults' interpersonal relationships." *Computers in Human Behavior*, 62, 594-601.

- Katz, R., & Crocker, E. T. (2015). "Selfies and photo messaging as visual conversation." *International Journal of Communication*, 9, 1861-1872.

### Close Friends & Audience Restriction
- Litt, E. (2012). "Knock, knock. Who's there? The imagined audience." *Journal of Broadcasting & Electronic Media*, 56(3), 330-345.
  - "Imagined audience" theory — users curate content based on perceived viewers
  - Close Friends feature reduces imagined audience → lowers creation barrier

- Marwick, A. E., & boyd, d. (2011). "I tweet honestly, I tweet passionately: Twitter users, context collapse, and the imagined audience." *New Media & Society*, 13(1), 114-133.

### BeReal
- Karizat, N., et al. (2024). "BeReal and the promise of authentic social media." *New Media & Society* (forthcoming).
  - Analysis of BeReal's no-likes, time-constrained design
  - "Authenticity through constraint"
